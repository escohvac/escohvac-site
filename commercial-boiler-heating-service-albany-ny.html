Seasonal Inspection

What a pre-winter heating check covers

Every building is different, but a seasonal check typically looks at:

  1. 1 Boiler operation, firing and safety controls
  2. 2 Circulating pumps, valves and visible piping
  3. 3 Thermostats, zone controls and building automation settings
  4. 4 Signs of leaks, corrosion or worn components
  5. 5 Overall performance, with a written summary of what we found and what we recommend

You get a clear picture of where your system stands, and a short list of anything that should be handled before heating season is in full swing.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

When should a commercial boiler be serviced?

Most commercial heating systems should be inspected every year before heating season starts. Fall is ideal, since it leaves time to order parts and make repairs before the weather turns. Buildings with critical operations or older equipment may benefit from more frequent checks, which is something we can build into a maintenance agreement.

What are the signs my boiler or heating system is having trouble?

Common warning signs include uneven heat between areas of the building, unusual noises, a system that turns on and off frequently, water around the boiler or piping, rising energy costs, and more service calls than usual. If you notice any of these, it's worth having a technician take a look before it turns into a shutdown.

Should I repair or replace an aging boiler?

It depends on the age and condition of the equipment, how often it needs repairs, the cost and availability of parts, and how efficiently it runs. We'll look at your system, explain your options plainly, and give you an honest recommendation, including when a repair is the smarter choice.

Can ESCO service equipment we already have, even if you didn't install it?

Yes. We service and repair commercial equipment from the major manufacturers whether or not we installed it.

Call our dispatch line at (518) 482-0375. 24/7 emergency dispatch is available for critical commercial accounts, and a maintenance agreement helps make sure we know your building before an emergency happens.
